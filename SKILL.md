## Panel-specific overlay

The transformed design is one small costumed character — the photograph's own subject with its head, silhouette and real colours still recognisable — standing upright and facing straight out, centred on an invisible horizon in a single clean warm-white field, drawn a size smaller than instinct with generous headroom above, no cast shadow and nothing behind it. It wears one plain single-colour costume shape (cape, hood or pointed hat) falling straight from the shoulders and slightly flared at the hem, in the photo's most emotional colour softened like washed cloth, and holds a little stick or one small prop turned from something in the photo, carrying the single warmest bright accent. Several subjects stand side by side in a row, different heights, each in its own costume; a scene keeps only one representative object or fragment. Paint in one or two layers of transparent watercolour with brushy edges and dried tide-marks, dry-brush for fur, opaque gouache dots for the prop; the face is only two small black dot eyes and a tiny dark nose or mouth, with no expression. The one tiny line of pencil handwriting sits centred below the feet with a clear gap, in the painting's darkest ink kept faint, deadpan and gently funny. Reject a full-scene copy, a painted background, ground or scenery, side-on or running poses, a costume that reads as ordinary clothing, big cartoon eyes, a cartoon look, detailed realistic fur, heavy shadows, black outlines, garish colour, and any part of the figure touching the fold.

These overlay rules add to `references/soldier-runtime.md`. They never replace the source brief or the family runtime contract.

Write every selected final PNG directly inside one fresh task directory under `~/Desktop/xxd/xxd-panel-347/` (or the explicit `--out` root). Use collision-safe filenames; do not create source, mode, or size subdirectories and do not generate an automatic contact sheet.
